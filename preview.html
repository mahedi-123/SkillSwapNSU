The exchange

Four steps, one status column

Every trade walks the same path, which is why a single status field on exchangerequests can describe the whole life cycle.

Browse students
01 / Pending

List what you can teach and what you want

Each skill is tagged Teach or Learn with a level from Beginner to Expert. Those two lists are the whole matching engine — a self-join on userskills finds people whose lists are the mirror of yours.

02 / Accepted

Send a trade, get an answer

A request names both halves at once: the skill you give and the skill you get. Your partner accepts or declines, and nothing can be scheduled until they do.

03 / Scheduled

Book the session

Pick a date, a time and a length. Online sessions carry a meeting link; offline ones carry a spot on campus. A unique key on the slot stops the same pair from double booking.

04 / Completed

Rate each other

Once a session is marked complete, both partners may leave one rating out of five. Averages are derived from those rows, never stored on the profile.

What you get

Built around the trade, not a marketplace

No wallet, no checkout, no listings that sit unanswered. Every screen is about two students and one agreement.

Two-way matching

The dashboard only suggests people who teach something on your learn list and want something on your teach list. Those requests get accepted far more often.

Search that narrows

Filter by skill, category, department, experience level and name at once, then sort by best match or highest rated.

Sessions with detail

Date, time, duration, online or offline, and where. Reschedule, complete or cancel from the same card.

Ratings that mean something

A review can only follow a completed session, and each partner may write exactly one. No drive-by scores.

Admin console

Manage students and the skill catalogue, moderate reviews, and watch referential rules block the deletes they should.

Raw SQL throughout

Six normalised tables, parameterised queries, no ORM. Every button names the statement it stands for.

The rule

No money moves. That is the whole point.

Tutoring costs money most students do not have, and plenty of them are already good at something worth trading. SkillSwap settles in skills, so the price of learning is teaching.

Submission

CSE311L Database Systems Lab

Six normalised tables in MySQL with primary keys, foreign keys, unique keys, CHECK constraints, indexes and three views, driven end to end by hand-written parameterised SQL.

This page is the static build on GitHub Pages, so the rows come from a JavaScript export of seed.sql. The same markup runs on Flask against MySQL for the final review.
